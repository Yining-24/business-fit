# Discovery, ideation and fit
Adapted and rewritten methods; attribution and changes: [sources](sources.md).

## Discover problems
Map communities, roles and workflows the user knows; distinguish real access from hoped-for access. A family business or school network may be an entry point, not guaranteed customers or permission to use its data.

Record each problem as: segment; task and trigger; current workaround; frequency; time/money/consequence; evidence source and date; payer versus user; access route; unknowns. Explore recent concrete incidents: 'What happened last time?', 'Show me how you handled it', 'What have you tried or paid for?' Avoid pitching and hypothetical 'Would you buy?' questions.

Rank promising problems by severity, recurrence, workaround effort, reachability and relevant experience. Label speculation explicitly; where there are no observations, propose discovery conversations rather than claiming findings. Limited access is a testable acquisition constraint, not automatic disqualification.

## Diverge, then converge
Frame the desired customer outcome and real constraints. Generate a manageable variety, usually 5–7 genuinely different approaches for a broad brainstorm, fewer for a narrow ask. Try analogies, combining existing services, removing steps, reversing a workflow, manual delivery and different payers. Temporarily relaxing a constraint may reveal alternatives, but restore actual constraints before recommending.

For each idea state customer, problem, offer, delivery method, payer, revenue model and route to a first customer. Include options that can earn through existing abilities without substantial development. Compare 2–3 worthwhile directions if supported; do not fill a quota with weak ideas. Capture promising alternatives and why others were set aside.

## Assess personal fit
For build / sell / operate, record demonstrated evidence, capability gaps, cost and time to close them, and dependencies. Consider:
- Build: independent competence versus AI-assisted prototyping; tools, supply and delivery quality.
- Sell: reachable buyers, credibility, willingness to do outreach, language, sales cycle and channel.
- Operate: weekly capacity, support, scheduling, fulfillment, recurring obligations and reliability.
- Personal return: cash at risk, time to first revenue, income goal, enjoyment and willingness to repeat the work.

Use 'fit now', 'fit after a bounded gap is closed', or 'poor fit under current constraints' with rationale. A missing mission-critical ability may rule out a model today; it does not rule out entrepreneurship. Do not require quitting a job, elite credentials, a cofounder or a durable moat for a small profitable operation. Counter your preferred idea with the strongest evidence against it; a fresh review can help, but changing chats does not remove the need for evidence.
