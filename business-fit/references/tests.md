# Assumptions and inexpensive tests
Adapted and rewritten methods; attribution and changes: [sources](sources.md).

Extract assumptions about pain, value, payer, price, acquisition, delivery, usability, repeat use, operating capacity and timing. For each record existing evidence, consequence if false and uncertainty (high/medium/low with reasons). Prioritize high consequence plus weak evidence; even a well-supported assumption may merit monitoring if failure is catastrophic. Identify any assumption that alone makes the current model untenable.

Choose an experiment that addresses the claim:
- Problem interviews or observation: reveal incidents and workarounds; do not establish willingness to pay.
- Mock offer/landing page with an honest availability statement: test message and channel; clicks and waitlists are interest signals.
- Existing tools or a manual concierge service: test delivery and value before automation.
- One-session service, pop-up, paid workshop or small-batch sample: test an actual bounded purchase and fulfillment.
- Transparent paid pilot or refundable preorder: test commitment, with delivery terms and refund handling. Payment is stronger evidence than praise but does not establish retention or profitability.
- Mockup usability test: test comprehension and use, not demand.

Prefer days and an affordable loss cap; adjust to the user's hours and sales cycle instead of enforcing a universal timeline. Do not misrepresent availability, automation, customers or results.

## Experiment card
- Assumption and falsifiable hypothesis: segment, action, price if relevant, deadline.
- Target customers and exclusions; recruitment channel, access assumptions and concrete steps.
- Offer/artifact and what is delivered manually or simulated.
- Duration, owner hours, cash cap, cost breakdown and stopping condition.
- Measurement: exposure → qualified leads → offers → commitments → payments → fulfillment/repeat use as relevant. Define denominator and qualification criteria; log refusals and reasons.
- Sample target and rationale: what directional decision it can support; do not claim statistical significance from a convenient small sample.
- Predeclared success, failure and inconclusive bands, with numbers where meaningful and reasons tied to economics or the decision. Distinguish low reach from rejection of the offer. Do not move thresholds after seeing results.
- Next decision for each outcome: bounded continuation, revised segment/price/channel, stop, or a specific further test.

After execution, report actual counts, costs, caveats and surprises; compare with the original criteria. Do not substitute simulated customers or AI judgments for observed outcomes. If recruitment fails, the channel is unproven; demand has not necessarily been disproved.
